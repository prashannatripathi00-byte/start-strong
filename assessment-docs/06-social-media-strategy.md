# 06 — Social Media Strategy

## Social Media Overview

Social media is a secondary traffic channel for Start Strong. Its primary roles are:
- Driving awareness and referral traffic to the website
- Building an engaged audience of beginner runners
- Amplifying blog content to reach new audiences
- Creating brand recognition for the Start Strong name

> **Current status:** Social media accounts have not yet been created. This document outlines the proposed strategy to be implemented once accounts are active. All follower counts, engagement rates and results are **proposed** — not actual outcomes.

---

## Platform Strategy

### Instagram

**Priority: High**

Instagram is the most visually aligned platform for fitness content and has the highest concentration of health and wellness audiences.

**Content themes:**
- Beginner running tips (carousel posts — 5–7 slides)
- Short motivational quotes with running imagery
- "Did you know?" facts about running and fitness
- Blog article promotion (headline + image + link in bio)
- Week-in-review: celebrate small wins

**Formats:**
- Feed posts: informational carousels, quote graphics
- Stories: polls, Q&As, quick tips
- Reels: 15–30 second beginner tips, run-with-me content

**Posting frequency (proposed):** 3–4 times per week

**Bio link:** Link to homepage or rotating key landing pages using a link aggregator (e.g. Linktree or direct URL)

---

### Facebook

**Priority: Medium**

Facebook is better suited to longer content, community building and reaching an older demographic (30–55) who are a strong part of the returning-runner audience.

**Content themes:**
- Shared blog articles with introductory context
- Running challenge posts
- Community questions ("What was your first run like?")
- Gear advice posts

**Formats:**
- Link posts (blog articles)
- Text posts (community questions)
- Photo posts with captions

**Posting frequency (proposed):** 2–3 times per week

---

### YouTube

**Priority: Medium (future)**

YouTube is the highest-impact long-term social channel for the running niche due to strong search intent within the platform itself.

**Proposed content:**
- "How to Start Running" beginner guide video
- Running plan walkthrough (Week 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Running shoe selection guide
- Q&A: "Your beginner running questions answered"

YouTube requires significantly more production effort than written content. This channel is proposed for later-stage development once the written content base is established.

---

### TikTok

**Priority: Low-Medium (review)**

TikTok reaches a younger demographic and has strong reach for short-form fitness content. Consider if the audience data supports it once Instagram is established.

## Content Planning

### Sample Post Concepts

These are proposed content ideas — not published posts.

**Instagram carousel: "5 Mistakes Every New Runner Makes"**
- Slide 1: Title card
- Slide 2: Going too fast
- Slide 3: Skipping warm-up
- Slide 4: Too much too soon
- Slide 5: Wrong footwear
- Slide 6: Trying to run every day
- Slide 7: CTA → link in bio for full article

**Instagram motivational post:**
- Image: running shoes on a path
- Caption: "The first run is the hardest. The second one is a little easier. By the tenth, you're hooked. Start today."
- CTA: "Save this post for when you need a reminder."

**Facebook article promotion:**
- Image: blog post featured image
- Text: 2–3 sentences introducing the article topic + key insight
- Link to article
- Question to drive comments: "What's your biggest challenge as a new runner?"

---

## Social Media and Website Connection

The website includes:
- Open Graph metadata for rich preview cards when articles are shared
- Social share buttons on every article (Facebook, Twitter/X, copy link)
- Footer social icons (links to accounts when created)
- Social-specific campaign landing pages (can be added as needed)

GA4 is configured to track `social_click` events from share buttons, allowing measurement of social engagement from the website.
