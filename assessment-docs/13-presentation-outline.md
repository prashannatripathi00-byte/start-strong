# 13 — Presentation Outline

## Start Strong — YouTube Presentation Guide

This document outlines a suggested structure for the recorded video presentation. Adapt it to your own delivery style and the available recording time.

> **Note:** The feedback template requests a YouTube link but does not specify a minimum or maximum presentation length. A focused, evidence-based presentation of 8–15 minutes is typically more effective than a padded one.

---

## Preparation Checklist

Before recording:

- [ ] Website is live at the public URL
- [ ] GA4 data is collecting and you have at least 1–2 weeks of data
- [ ] Google Search Console is connected
- [ ] Screenshots of analytics are prepared
- [ ] Browser is set to full-screen mode for demos
- [ ] Assessment documents are reviewed
- [ ] Cookie consent has been accepted on your device so GA is active during the demo

---

## Presentation Structure

### Section 1 — Introduction (1–2 minutes)

**Cover:**
- Your name and Banner ID
- The name of the website: Start Strong
- The tagline: "Your Running Journey Starts Here"
- The website URL
- Brief overview: what Start Strong is and who it is for

**Sample opening:**

> "This is my Digital Marketing Project presentation. My website is called Start Strong — a beginner-friendly running and fitness platform designed to help people who have never run before take their first steps. The site is live at [URL] and was built using Astro and hosted on GitHub Pages."

---

### Section 2 — Market Background and Topic Justification (1–2 minutes)

**Cover:**
- Why running as a niche
- Target audience description
- Market opportunity (without inventing statistics)
- The gap Start Strong fills

---

### Section 3 — Website Structure Walkthrough (2–3 minutes)

**Live demo of the website. Cover:**

1. Homepage — hero section, value proposition, category overview
2. Start Running page — training plan structure
3. Blog page — category filter and search
4. One article — show full article with related content, share buttons
5. Running Gear page — gear comparison approach
6. About page — mission, editorial standards
7. Contact and Privacy Policy

**Key points to emphasise:**
- Responsive design (resize browser or show mobile view)
- Navigation — works on desktop and mobile
- Consistent branding throughout
- Internal linking between sections

---

### Section 4 — Content Strategy (1 minute)

**Cover:**
- The four initial articles and why they were chosen
- How Markdown content collections work (adding articles without duplicating code)
- Content categories and their search intent alignment

---

### Section 5 — Digital Marketing Mix (1–2 minutes)

**Cover:**
- Product: free educational content
- Place: organic search, social media, direct traffic
- Promotion: SEO, social media, proposed PPC
- Price: free access
- Reference your digital marketing mix document

---

### Section 6 — SEO Implementation (1–2 minutes)

**Cover:**
- Technical SEO: sitemap, robots.txt, canonical URLs
- On-page SEO: unique titles, descriptions, heading structure
- Open Graph and structured data
- Show the sitemap in the browser
- Keyword strategy overview

**Show in the browser:**
- Page source of one article (title tag, meta description, OG tags)
- Sitemap URL

---

### Section 7 — Social Media Strategy (30–60 seconds)

**Cover:**
- Proposed platforms: Instagram (primary), Facebook, YouTube
- Content themes and formats
- Social share buttons on articles
- Current status: accounts to be created (if not yet live)

---

### Section 8 — PPC and Affiliate Proposals (1 minute)

**Cover:**
- PPC: proposed Google Ads campaign targeting beginner running queries
- Key proposed ad copy examples
- Proposed budget and rationale
- Affiliate: proposed strategy, disclosure requirement, no current active agreements

Emphasise these are proposals — not executed campaigns.

---

### Section 9 — Google Analytics Findings (2–3 minutes)

**This is the most heavily assessed section. Use real data only.**

**Cover:**
- Show GA4 Acquisition report (where traffic comes from)
- Show top pages by engagement
- Show event data (CTA clicks, article engagement)
- Show mobile vs desktop breakdown
- Discuss any Search Console data available

**If data is limited:**

> "The site has been live for [X days/weeks]. The data collected so far shows [specific real observation]. While the traffic volume is limited at this early stage, the data indicates [specific insight]."

Acknowledge limitations honestly — limited data is expected for a new website. Do not invent numbers.

---

### Section 10 — Website Improvements (1 minute)

**Cover:**
- At least one real improvement made based on observed data
- What you observed → what you changed → result (if available)

Reference your improvement log in document 11.

---

### Section 11 — Conclusions and Future Development (1 minute)

**Cover:**
- What has been achieved
- What the data suggests works well
- Limitations encountered
- Three realistic next steps
- Long-term vision for Start Strong

## Presentation Tips

- Keep the live demo moving — avoid long pauses while pages load
- Point out specific features directly rather than describing them abstractly
- Use real data even if the numbers are small — honesty is better than approximation
- For proposed elements (PPC, affiliate, social), be explicit that they are proposals
- Keep answers to criteria concise — cover all points without padding
